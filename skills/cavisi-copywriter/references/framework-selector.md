# Copy Framework Selector

Use a framework only when it improves the requested artifact. Do not expose the framework name unless the user asks for analysis.

## Short social copy

Use `Hook -> Value -> CTA`: a relevant observation or question, one explanation grounded in approved facts, and one concrete next action.

## Educational or trust-building copy

Use `Observation -> Clarification -> Evidence/limit -> CTA`.

This is the preferred Cavisi structure for explaining Scalp-first, product roles, sources, limitations, or common misunderstandings.

## Product-role copy

Use a restrained form of `Feature -> Role -> Reader value`:

- State the approved product or format fact.
- Explain its role without upgrading it into an outcome.
- Explain why understanding that role helps the reader make a clearer decision.

## Problem-aware copy

Use a safe form of PAS:

- Problem: describe confusion or a practical difficulty.
- Clarification: explain why it occurs without intensifying fear.
- Solution: provide a clear next step or decision aid.

Do not agitate illness, permanent damage, appearance shame, wasted identity, or social rejection.

## Before-after reasoning

Use `Current understanding -> Clearer understanding -> Bridge`, not unverified product transformation.

The “after” state may be better clarity, an easier decision, or understanding the two roles. It must not imply a physical result without evidence.

## Storytelling

Use only supplied, authorized, or clearly fictionalized scenarios. Never present a composite or invented story as a real customer experience.

Preferred structure: `Ordinary moment -> Real question -> Useful reframe -> Practical next step`.

## Comparison

Use `Criterion -> Difference -> Relevance -> Limit`. Compare roles, evidence levels, formats, or decision criteria. Do not disparage competitors or invent comparative superiority.

## Batch and repurposing

Treat approved pillar content as the source of truth. Adapt hierarchy, length, pacing, and CTA without creating new claims.

## Selection rules

- One content unit uses one dominant structure.
- A hook does not need every argument.
- A framework must never force unsupported proof, urgency, outcomes, or testimonials.
- If the brief is structurally complete, preserve it instead of imposing a formula.
